**You do not need to choose between constant WhatsApp checking and disappearing from your relationships.** A workable digital detox separates urgent reachability from routine conversation: calls and selected alerts can interrupt you, while everything else waits for a scheduled check or digest.

The mistake is trying to keep the same notification system while merely promising yourself that you will look less often.

## Start with an emergency path

Before changing devices, tell the people who may genuinely need you:

1. Call my mobile number for anything urgent.
2. If I do not answer, call a second time.
3. Use one agreed phrase, such as "urgent today," when a text needs a fast response.

Test this with at least one family member. AI filtering and notification rules can reduce noise, but they should not be the only path for health, safety, childcare, or other critical situations.

## Choose your level of separation

| Detox setup | Where WhatsApp lives | Interruptions while away | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Scheduled checking | Smartphone in a drawer | Calls and SMS only | A first experiment |
| Computer-only WhatsApp | Desktop or web session | Calls and SMS only | People who work near a computer |
| Dumbphone plus Lightfriend | Separate WhatsApp setup device; selected access over SMS | Focused alerts, digests, calls, and SMS | People who need limited WhatsApp reachability on the move |

Start with the least complicated setup that solves your problem. You can add a bridge only if computer-only access leaves a real gap.

## Define what deserves an interruption

Write the rule before you configure the tool. Useful criteria include:

- messages from a partner, child, caregiver, or manager;
- a direct question that needs an answer today;
- a message that explicitly mentions you;
- a temporary event, such as a delivery, travel change, or medical update;
- repeated attempts to reach you across more than one channel.

"Anything that sounds important" is too broad. Name the people, time window, and type of situation.

## Put routine conversation into batches

Family and work groups can produce dozens of low-stakes messages around one useful update. A scheduled digest creates a deliberate review point without recreating a live feed over SMS.

For example:

- urgent direct messages can arrive immediately;
- routine direct messages can wait until lunch and early evening;
- group chats can be summarized once per day;
- a noisy event group can be excluded until you ask about it.

Lightfriend supports [intentional notifications](/blog/intentional-notifications) and [message digests](/blog/message-digests). Summaries can omit nuance, so ask for the original context before acting on something consequential.

## A 30-minute setup

1. Write down five people who need a fast path to you.
2. Give them your call-and-SMS emergency instructions.
3. Move the smartphone out of the room where you sleep or work.
4. Pick one or two times to review routine WhatsApp messages.
5. If needed, configure focused Lightfriend alerts for the five people.
6. Configure one daily group-chat digest instead of immediate forwarding.
7. Send test messages that should and should not trigger an alert.

## What Lightfriend changes—and what it does not

Lightfriend gives a basic phone a smaller interface to connected messages. You can ask what someone said, search recent context, reply, receive focused alerts, and request summaries without opening a feed.

It does not reproduce every WhatsApp feature. Rich media and provider-specific features vary, and WhatsApp calls do not become native dumbphone calls. Initial pairing requires the native WhatsApp app, and linked access can require periodic maintenance or re-pairing.

Messages delivered as SMS also pass through your cellular carrier. Treat SMS as a convenient transport, not as an end-to-end encrypted continuation of a WhatsApp conversation.

## Measure whether the detox is working

After one week, ask:

- Did anyone fail to reach me when it truly mattered?
- How many alerts were actually urgent?
- Did I open the backup smartphone outside the planned windows?
- Do I need fewer alerts, or a better emergency rule?
- Am I spending more uninterrupted time on the reason I started the detox?

If every WhatsApp message still reaches you immediately, you changed the screen but not the interruption system. The useful outcome is selective reachability: the people who matter can reach you, while the feed no longer decides when you look.
