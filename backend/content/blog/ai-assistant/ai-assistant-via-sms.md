Most assistants begin with an app. That makes sense technically, but it also puts the assistant beside every feed, badge, and habit you were trying to leave behind.

Lightfriend starts somewhere simpler: an ordinary phone call or text message. You can ask for information, check recent conversations, and respond through the communication tools already built into almost every phone.

## The interface changes the habit

SMS is intentionally limited. There is no infinite canvas, algorithmic feed, or row of recommendations waiting after the answer. You ask for something, receive a compact response, and put the phone away.

That constraint is the point. Lightfriend is not trying to reproduce a smartphone inside a text thread. It is meant to preserve access to people and a few useful actions while making distraction less convenient.

## Connected, not constantly checking

When supported services are connected, Lightfriend can bring recent WhatsApp, Signal, Telegram, and email context into the conversation. It can also screen incoming messages and collect routine items into scheduled digests.

Those tools do not guarantee that every message is classified perfectly or that an external provider is always available. They create a smaller surface for communication: fewer checks, fewer interruptions, and a direct way to ask when you actually need context.

The aim is not more time with an assistant. It is enough assistance to spend less time with a screen.
